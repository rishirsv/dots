# Plan visible product changes

Read this when a plan changes a visible interaction, uses product references,
or must resolve presentation.

## Define the experience

Specify what the person sees first, where the primary action lives, how they
open detail, which part of the system creates or changes records, and what
context survives after dismissal. Cover only states the product can reach,
including relevant empty, loading, unavailable, failed, long-content,
localization, accessibility, focus, and compact-layout behavior.

When presentation is unresolved, use the relevant product-design workflow to
develop two or three genuinely different models with consistent scope and
representative data. The user selects when the alternatives change product
behavior or outcome. Record the user's choice and why the other options lose.
Show that direction at enough fidelity to make hierarchy, actions, states, and
adaptation reviewable.

## Use references as evidence

For each inspiration or product reference, name the one contribution that
changes the decision: hierarchy, interaction, continuity, density, disclosure,
or material treatment. Do not copy its appearance without explaining the fit or
present inspiration as evidence of current implementation.

Label real screenshots, generated concepts, and proposed mocks clearly. A mock
must answer a design question; it must not imply an undefined goal, eligibility
rule, trend, or time ordering. Create static product mocks
only for HTML output or through another requested workflow that can render and
verify them.

## Readiness

- Is the primary behavior and owner explicit?
- Are the relevant states, accessibility, adaptation, dismissal, refresh, and
  focus behavior defined?
- If presentation was unresolved, did the user select among meaningfully
  different models?
- Does every visual match the chosen behavior?

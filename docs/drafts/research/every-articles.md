## AI Style Guides: How to Help AI Write Like You

> “A style guide is not a prompt.”

### Core claim

Unguided models converge on safe, average prose. An AI style guide is a reusable
operating manual for reproducing a writer's judgment, while a prompt specifies
the current task. Describing a voice with flattering adjectives is too vague to
change an actual draft.

### Style-guide contents

1. Voice and tone expressed as tensions and boundaries.
2. Characteristic openings, argument movement, pacing, and endings.
3. Sentence rhythm, length, punctuation, diction, and abstraction tolerance.
4. Signature moves such as scene-to-idea transitions, humor, or reframing.
5. Anti-patterns paired with corrections.
6. Positive annotated examples.
7. Negative annotated examples.
8. A revision checklist based on recurring model failures.

The blacklist may be the highest-value component because generic AI habits are
often easier to identify and remove than an entire voice is to define.

### Example editorial frameworks

The single-author *Working Overtime* guide defines voice through tensions:
participant and observer, enthusiast and skeptic, conversational and rigorous,
optimistic without naivete, and critical without cynicism.

Its recurring essay arc is:

1. Begin with friction or stakes.
2. Show experimentation, failures, and the messy middle.
3. Surface a broader cultural, historical, or philosophical pattern.
4. Deliver a useful framework, workflow, prompt, or reframe.

Named signature moves include Friction to Zoom Out to Framework, Adventure
Narrative, and Borrowed Lens. Revision checks ask whether stakes arrive early,
the process feels lived, technical terms are explained, transformation is
visible, and the ending does more than summarize.

The multi-author *Source Code* guide permits a voice spectrum while maintaining
named structural templates: Discovery Arc, Technical Deep-Dive, Workshop
Report, Multi-Case-Study, Gateway Guide, Two-Part Series, and Q&A. It expects a
memorable concept, personal stakes, newcomer-readable explanations, and
concrete evidence such as numbers, named tools, outcomes, and observable
failures.

### Maturity model

- A starter guide has three to five precise voice rules, a preferred structure,
  five to ten prohibited patterns, and two or three annotated examples.
- A working guide adds sentence examples, named signature moves, revision
  checks, structural templates, and before-and-after anti-patterns. It becomes
  persistent project context.
- A compound system checks every draft automatically and feeds repeated
  editorial corrections back into the system as new guardrails.

The mature Every guides took months of daily use and revision.

### Construction workflow

1. Supply three to five representative or aspirational samples.
2. Let the AI interview the writer instead of drafting the guide immediately.
3. Ask one question at a time about tone, structure, rhythm, openings,
   metaphors, habits, dislikes, and generic failure.
4. Use contrastive sentence examples because reaction is more precise than
   abstract self-description.
5. Synthesize the evidence into the guide.
6. Use the guide for about a week.
7. Record repeated corrections, sharpen vague instructions, and keep only rules
   that noticeably improve output.

The guide should optimize for real writing tasks, not every conceivable format.
It must leave room for variation and surprise, and it cannot replace editorial
taste.

## After Automation

> “The frame is not the framer.”

### Core claim

AI makes yesterday's recorded competence cheap. More output creates more
sameness, which increases the value of current, situated human judgment.
Automation therefore creates new expert work around framing, reviewing,
differentiating, and maintaining AI systems.

### Two modes of agent work

Agent employees receive asynchronous assignments or own stable portions of a
workflow. Human-agent collaboration instead puts the person and agents in one
workspace: the human plans, delegates, inspects, interrupts, redirects, and
extends the work.

The article calls the latter pattern the human sandwich: a person frames the
task, AI collapses the framed work, and a person judges and extends the result.
In writing, Shipper composes while agents supply paragraph drafts, research,
and copy editing. The interaction is not a one-shot handoff.

### Why ownership remains human

Agents still require someone to select the problem, define quality, catch
errors, turn output into a real decision or process, and maintain the agent.
Every found that lightly maintained personal agents became stale and moved
toward explicit ownership and engineering support.

The article's economic argument is:

1. Models package the visible residue of completed human work.
2. Previously rare competence becomes widely available.
3. Output volume rises.
4. Shared model defaults create recognizable sameness.
5. Audiences raise their standards and value work fitted to a specific person,
   company, customer, codebase, or moment.
6. Experts move to the new edge by building review queues, evaluations,
   harnesses, instructions, permissions, and workflows.
7. Models later absorb some of that framed work and the human edge moves again.

A corpus records what worked previously; it does not know the writer's current
purpose. Voice state can encode past judgment, but the writer must still frame
what matters now.

## Writing With AI Is Harder Than You Think

> “It takes rigor, judgment, and willingness to be told your work isn’t good enough.”

### Core claim

Serious AI-assisted writing remains an iterative practice of thinking,
negotiating structure, drafting, rejecting, revising, testing, and applying
taste. Faster first drafts shift more responsibility onto judgment.

The author's workflow evolved from manual prompt-and-paste drafting, through
uploaded past work and a style guide, into a dedicated writing agent with
explicit phases, specialized critics, style checks, AI-pattern detection, and
sentence-level editing.

### Complete workflow

#### 1. Brainstorm through an interview

The agent asks why the topic matters now, where it appears in the writer's
experience, what the reader should leave thinking, where the personal friction
is, and whether disagreement concerns facts, reasoning, or the whole frame.
Difficulty answering reveals that an idea may not be ready. The writer may
reject questions that point toward the wrong argument.

#### 2. Negotiate the outline

The agent proposes a structure from the interview. The writer reorders
sections, removes thin beats, adds missing ideas, relocates anecdotes, and
rejects mechanical mappings until the outline matches the intended mental
shape. Structure is negotiated, not delegated cognition.

#### 3. Draft section by section

The agent drafts small units from the approved outline. Near-matches are
roughened, rephrased, broken apart, or given characteristic asides. Weak
sections are discarded and rewritten from the intended feeling. Sections move
when their purpose becomes clearer, and technically adequate but personally
inauthentic analogies are replaced. Authorship lives in the continuing choices
about what stays, moves, changes, and disappears.

#### 4. Run an adversarial reviewer panel

- Hemingway pressures economy and removes expendable language.
- Hitchcock examines pacing, tension, and reasons to continue.
- Mom reader checks accessibility to a general audience.
- Asshole reader attacks weak logic and unsupported claims.
- Sedaris looks for humor and visible personality.

The writer adjudicates every critique. Some feedback identifies a real defect;
other feedback attempts to turn the work into a different piece and should be
rejected.

#### 5. Finalize through distinct checks

Only after the substance works does the author run an AI-pattern check, a
mechanical style check, a line edit, and a final personal-voice pass. She still
reads aloud, searches for exact words, evaluates rhythm, tests whether the work
merits attention, and asks whether vulnerability is earned.

## Cross-Article Implications

### Four separate kinds of state

1. **Global voice state:** stable tensions, sentence tendencies, argument
   habits, signature moves, anti-patterns, and exemplars.
2. **Context profile:** channel, audience, relationship, formality, emotional
   temperature, typical structure, vocabulary, greetings, closings, humor, and
   directness.
3. **Working-document state:** purpose, stakes, thesis, audience, sources,
   outline, draft history, open questions, accepted and rejected directions,
   and unresolved review findings.
4. **Learning state:** corrections, reactions, provenance, confidence, scope,
   versions, and candidate preferences awaiting confirmation.

This separation prevents an iMessage habit from contaminating a report and
prevents a long-running report from being treated as a one-shot prompt.

### Evidence and reaction

Start with three to five representative samples per important context. Tag each
sample by channel, audience, date, draft stage, and whether the user considers
it representative or aspirational.

Extract both quantitative and operational layers. Quantitative stylometry may
include sentence and paragraph length, function words, contractions,
punctuation, lexical diversity, hedging, intensifiers, pronouns, questions,
fragments, and recurring n-grams. Operational style includes openings,
information order, argument movement, rhythm, metaphor, emotional temperature,
signature moves, anti-patterns, and endings.

The Every articles prescribe the operational layer. Statistical stylometry is
an enhancement, not a claim made by the sources.

Then interview contrastively: present short alternatives, ask which feels
right, and capture why. Self-description alone is too vague.

### Hierarchical routing

Route each request through:

1. Stable personal invariants.
2. The best-matching context profile.
3. Current audience and relationship.
4. Current task or project state.
5. Explicit instructions for this turn.

When a context lacks evidence, inherit only high-confidence global traits and
ask narrow questions or offer variants.

### Process depth by artifact

A short message needs intent, recipient, desired effect, a few variants, and a
light context/voice check. An email adds relationship, required facts, tone,
greeting, directness, and closing conventions. A long report needs durable
project state, an interview, a negotiated outline, section-level drafting,
source and open-question tracking, and separate structural, argument, audience,
style, and line-edit reviews.

### Separate operations

- Generate new prose from intent, sources, voice, and context.
- Voice edit while preserving meaning.
- Developmentally edit structure, evidence, and argument without treating those
  changes as voice preferences.
- Line edit without flattening signature rhythm.
- Run independent critic passes that the user adjudicates.
- Scrub generic AI patterns, check mechanical rules, and verify voice.

### Conservative learning

After a user revision, compare the draft with accepted text, classify the
likely preference, and record it as a candidate rule. Promote it immediately
only when the user explicitly names a standing preference; otherwise require
recurrence. Assign it to the narrowest valid context, preserve conflicts, keep
version history, and allow rollback. One rushed edit should not redefine a
person's voice.

### Human sandwich

At the start, the user frames purpose, stakes, audience, and desired effect.
During the work, the system exposes consequential outline and editorial
decisions. At the end, the user judges, rejects, and extends the result, and the
system captures those choices as evidence. The target is a stateful
collaborator, not autonomous impersonation.

## Source Gaps To Resolve Ourselves

- The articles do not define statistical stylometry, sample sufficiency,
  confidence calibration, or evaluation metrics.
- They do not specify when an edit is persistent, accidental, editor-imposed,
  project-specific, or context-specific.
- Published samples may contain substantial editing by others; native voice and
  house style need separate provenance.
- Deliberate voice evolution, aspirational style, aging evidence, conflicting
  eras, and rollback require an explicit policy.
- Privacy, consent, retention, deletion, sensitive-source handling, and
  connector access are unspecified.
- Automated checks can harden into a new sameness machine unless surprise and
  variation remain valid.
- The detailed workflow is optimized for essays; messaging channels need a
  compressed process.
- The articles offer no blind voice-match evaluation or context-routing
  benchmark.

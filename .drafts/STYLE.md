# Writing style guide — provisional

This first pass is drawn from one user-supplied passage about human wants and agency (`examples/agency-passage.md`). It describes a promising shape for reflective essays and vision pieces. It does not establish the writer's views on other subjects or a required structure for every format. Revise it after the trial pieces and the writer's feedback.

## Reader promise

Help the reader recognize a human need or obstacle, understand why it persists, and see a concrete possibility that follows from the argument. The piece should give more than encouragement: it should change what the reader can notice, question, or do.

## Argument and structure

- Start with a recognizable human situation or a direct claim that creates tension. Explain why the situation matters before widening the lens.
- Show the mechanism between desire and outcome. In the sample, forms, gatekeepers, schedules, and small unmade moves turn a want into an unrealized one.
- Make the stakes explicit, then offer a possibility that addresses the mechanism. Do not jump from the problem to an unearned solution.
- A useful shape for a short vision essay is: ordinary experience → hidden obstacle → human cost → plausible intervention → larger implication. Use another shape when the assignment calls for it.
- End by extending the thought into a question, possibility, or action. Avoid repeating the thesis as a closing slogan.

## Evidence and claim discipline

- Treat the supplied passage as evidence of writing preferences, not proof of factual claims. Its claims about human behavior and global scale are rhetorical and would need support in reported work.
- Keep observed facts, personal experience, hypothetical scenes, and forecasts distinct. Verify numbers, historical claims, product capabilities, and health or financial assertions before publication.
- A vivid example should illuminate the mechanism, not stand in for evidence about everyone.
- Surface a meaningful limitation when it changes the promise of the idea. Do not bury practical barriers beneath inspiration.

## Scope to test

The sample suggests interest in human agency, tools that help people act, and the gap between aspiration and institutions. These are hypotheses for the trial assignments, not permanent topic boundaries or positions attributed to the writer.

## Ready for review when

- The central idea is clear and the reader can follow each turn of the reasoning.
- The concrete examples actually support the point they illustrate.
- The intervention or payoff addresses the obstacle the piece named.
- Consequential factual claims have sources or are clearly marked as questions or possibilities.
- The ending adds something earned.
- The draft has been checked against `VOICE.md` without copying the sample's wording.
